# Rebuttal — Defense

**1. Data source. Survives in part.** Relying on yfinance is a real, named risk. But the claim that the fallback "cannot be used" goes further than the record. The record says only that its licence "forbids showing its prices to end users". It does not say whether simulated results built from those prices count. That is a risk to manage. It is not proof the launch fails.

**2. Retention unproven. Weak as a charge.** Every product has "no users yet" before it launches. The founder made the "second backtest" share the kill criterion so that launching would test retention. The plan already targets the slow first run ("under 60 seconds") and the retention features. Retention cannot be proven before launch.

**3. Capped front door. Survives in part.** The 50-a-day cap and the spam risk are real. The founder wrote both down. The planned no-signup trial puts the first backtest before signup, so the cap limits accounts, not first use. The record does not show whether the trial will ship before launch.

**4. Free tiers, no money plan. Does not defeat this case.** The case is to launch a product people come back to, not a business. The record says the app is "built to stay inside" free limits "using caching and hard rate limits." So growth would hit capacity before it hits the budget. The real cost is turned-away users, and that folds into point 3.

**The point to decide:** point 2. Does launching with retention unproven, but measured by a kill criterion the founder set in advance, mean the case "fails as stated"? Or is that exactly how this case should go ahead?
