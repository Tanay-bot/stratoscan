# Rebuttal — Prosecution

1. **Cost: survives only in part.** A $2–3 server makes a trial cheap, but the case is a product people come back to, not a cheap trial. The defense concedes the record says nothing about costs once usage outgrows the free tiers, and every service is on a free plan.

2. **Kill criterion: does not carry the case.** The record names the metric (second backtest, day-2 and day-7 return) but gives no threshold that would count as failure. And with no distribution plan and no target user, there may be no users to measure.

3. **Verification: survives, but beside the point.** 14,294 of 14,294 checks show the numbers are correct. They show nothing about whether anyone returns. The figures also rest on yfinance, an unofficial source whose licensed replacement is untested.

4. **Value before signup: fails as stated.** This is a plan, not a feature. The record lists "no-signup trials and shareable result links" as "still ahead", and today a first backtest "can take minutes on cold data." The defense's only way for the product to spread does not exist yet.

5. **Risks named: fails.** Writing a risk down does not fix it. Email deliverability is "not yet resolved." The yfinance alternative is untested, and its licence forbids showing its prices to end users. The 50-a-day signup cap still stands.

**The point to decide:** can this product, as described, reach anyone and bring them back? The one acquisition channel (share links) is unbuilt. The features meant to bring people back are still on the to-do list. No distribution plan or target user is on record. Launching now tests the gaps, not the product.
