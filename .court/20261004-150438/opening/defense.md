# Opening — Defense

The charge fails. This launch has its own tests built in, and the costly work is already done.

1. **Near-zero cost makes launching the cheapest experiment.** Record: "one small always-on server planned at about **$2–3 a month**"; "The screener makes zero outside calls per query." The objection is that there is no revenue model. At this cost, the product does not need revenue to survive a trial. Launching is how the gaps the record admits get filled: who the users are, and whether anyone wants it.

2. **There is a falsifiable success test.** Record: the kill criterion is "the share of users who run a **second backtest**" ("the single most important number in the product"), plus day-2 and day-7 return. "Come back to" is a claim that can be measured, and the founder says in advance what would disprove it. This answers the lack of user research: launching produces that research.

3. **The core claim is verified.** Record: "14,294 of 14,294 checks passing"; a frozen reference set "fails the build if the simulation math ever changes"; "around 49 automated test suites" on the server and 240 on the frontend. The objection is a fast, AI-assisted build by a founder with limited coding ability. Independent re-derivation of every number answers that better than credentials would.

4. **The plan puts value before signup.** Record: "let a stranger run a real backtest *without* an account, and share the result as a link." This works around the 50-signups-a-day cap and gives the product a way to spread.

5. **The risks are already named.** yfinance, the email cap, deliverability and regulation are "already written down", and the disclaimer is shown "on every result".

**Concessions:** the record has no distribution plan, no target user and no plan for costs beyond the free tiers. The goal of a backtest in "under 60 seconds" is still not met. The yfinance licensing risk is unresolved. These are work for the remaining plan days, not reasons to cancel.
