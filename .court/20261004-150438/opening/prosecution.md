# Opening — Prosecution

The charge: the case, "launch Stratoscan ... as a product people will come back to," fails as stated.

**1. The verification claim rests on data the founder cannot rely on.**
The founder says "Verification is the position" and every number must be correct. But price history "comes from **yfinance**, an unofficial library that reads Yahoo Finance." The only fallback "has been prepared but not tested against the live service; its licence forbids showing its prices to end users." A backtester has to show prices to end users. Cost: if Yahoo changes or blocks access, the core feature stops working and no licensed replacement can be used. The 14,294 passing checks prove the arithmetic is right. They do not prove the source data can be trusted or kept.

**2. "Come back to" is the goal, and nothing in the record supports it.**
The founder's own kill criterion is "the share of users who run a **second backtest**." Yet "today it can take minutes on cold data," and the fixes ("no-signup trials and shareable result links; templates; retention features") are all "still ahead." There are "No users yet," and the record contains no "user research, interviews, waitlist, or early interest." Cost: the founder launches with retention unproven and the first experience slow, which is exactly the condition under which a second backtest is least likely.

**3. The front door is capped and may be invisible.**
"Signup is capped at 50 per day site-wide," and "verification emails may land in spam. Not yet resolved." The no-signup trial meant to get around this is not built. Cost: any spike of interest, which is the only distribution channel named ("shareable result links"), hits a 50-person ceiling, and some of those 50 never see the verification email.

**4. "Free, forever" on free tiers, with no plan for success.**
"Every external service is on a free plan," there is "no paid tier," and the record does not say "how the product will ever make money" or "what happens to running costs if usage grows beyond the free tiers." Cost: growth, the very outcome being sought, breaks either the budget or the "free, forever" promise. Either one damages the trust the product is built on.
